# "Rules Horror" Script Formula

Based on 8 scripts from the reference channel: the cruise video I transcribed, plus the 7 you pasted.

| Video | Views | Length | Rules | Mode |

**Length doesn't decide views.** A 4-minute video and a 13-minute video both reached about 3.2M. The concept decides.

---

## 1. The title

**`If Your [ordinary, trusted thing] Ever [does one small wrong thing]… [COMMAND IN CAPS]`**

- The "thing" is something every viewer has: a dog, mom, a sibling, their bed, a teacher, the cinema, Google Maps.
- The wrong thing is small and specific, never "attacks you". Examples: says goodnight *twice*, stands on *two legs*, laughs at the *wrong part*.
- The command is 1–3 words in caps: ACT NORMAL, LIE, LOCK YOUR DOOR, DON'T.
- The top two videos (dog, mom) are about the *closest, most loved* things in the house. Closer to home means more views.

## 2. The opening (first 15–60 seconds)

These lines appear almost word for word in most of the videos:

1. **The rule as a direct order, followed by three "don'ts":**
   > "If your mom ever says good night to you twice, lock your door. Don't call out to her. Don't turn on your lamp. And whatever you do, don't say good night back."
2. **Ruling out the normal explanation:**
   > "I'm not talking about [normal thing]. I'm not talking about [normal thing]. I'm talking about **this**."
3. **The scene,** in present tense with physical detail (warm sheets, no footsteps, the voice right outside the door).
4. **A test the viewer can do:** "Watch their eyes. If they don't blink…" / "Count to five." / "Feel the mattress. If it dips…"
5. **The threat in one line:** "One of those good nights was your mom. The other one was something that's been listening to her for a very long time."
6. **The rule count plus a curiosity hook:**
   > "There are seven rules. Most people break the third. Nobody was ever supposed to need the seventh."
   > "Pay close attention to the third one, because it's the one most people break."

The 3.3M dog video reaches Rule 1 by 0:32 and the Google video by 0:17. **Short openings work.**

## 3. Each rule section (about 45–90 seconds each)

```
"Rule number X. [Command]."
→ This is the one you'll want to break: "This is going to be the hardest one."
→ WHY: the creature's logic ("A name can't be copied. A name has to be given.")
→ What it will try next (escalating)
→ IF/THEN contingency: "If it stops talking halfway through, freeze."
→ Short sting line that ends the section
```

- **Every creature has strict rules of its own.** "Only something that isn't allowed in has to ask." "It only works in daylight. So it brought some." That logic is what makes the rules feel real.
- **Signs that something is wrong:** a smile too wide, never blinking, moving without a sound, words that are right but "the spaces between them are not".
- **Each rule raises the stakes.** Early rules: stay still or stay quiet. Middle rules: it's testing you. Late rules: get out, hold a door, run.

## 4. The case story (around 45–65% of the way through)

Every manual-style video has one short true-sounding story about someone who broke a rule:
- "One teenager once reported…" / "There's a story people tell about this…" / "There was a boy named Ethan…" / "Case file 041. A man in Ohio…"
- It ends on **one quiet, chilling detail** instead of gore: the brother "answered twice"; four cereal bowls for a family of three; Biscuit died four months earlier.

## 5. Plants (what makes it stick)

Ask the viewer about **their own real life**, then pay it off later:
- "Have you ever woken up with a sore throat for no reason?… **Keep that in mind.**"
- "Have you ever asked your parents where your sibling's name came from?… **Hold on to that.**"
- "Think about the last time you climbed into a bed so cold it made you shiver… **That's going to matter.**"

## 6. The twist (last 15–20%)

The ending changes the meaning of everything before it:
- The bed: a *cold* bed is the dangerous one.
- The sibling: *you* invented your sibling.
- Mom: it doesn't want in, it wants to *be you*.
- The dog: the narrator is the dogs.
- The theater: the audience are earlier victims.
- Google Maps: a lonely son scrolling back to see his late mother waving.
- The cruise: the captain was a 12-year-old in your cabin in 1979.

The strongest twists carry **love or grief** (mom, dead dog, mother waving, "every morning you came back").

## 7. The ending

Repeat the opening line, then flip it, and finish on 2–6 words:
> "So if your bed is ever warm… get out slowly. **But if your bed is ever cold when you get in, don't turn around.** It's already standing right behind you."

Final lines from the channel: "Stay." / "Both arms." / "We're saving your seat." / "You're the one who made it up." / "He's trying to get out."

## 8. Voice and style

- **Second person, present or future tense:** "You'll hear…", "You get up and look through the peephole."
- **Short sentences and fragments:** "Not a little warm. Body warm."
- **Triads:** "Don't X. Don't Y. And whatever you do, don't Z." / "First the way you stand, then the way you walk, then your face."
- **Exact numbers:** 2:13 AM, 11:40, 406 times, 7 years, 30,000 years, case file 041.
- **An official narrator voice in some videos:** "We are a trusted government agency." "Thank you for your cooperation."
- **Dry jokes, used sparingly** (the 3.3M video is horror-comedy): "Do not say the word walk. We will not be saying it again." "You bought it a little raincoat."
- **Pace:** about 150–160 spoken words per minute, with a new escalation every 20–40 seconds.
- **YouTube chapters:** one chapter per rule, plus "Intro" and the case story. They use these in their descriptions.

## 9. Two formats

- **A. Manual** (mom, sibling, bed, dog, theater): "If this happens, you'll… Do not…". The viewer imagines it. Cheaper to animate because there are fewer locations.
- **B. Story through the rules** (cruise, Google Maps, substitute): "you" live through one night, and the rules arrive inside the story (a card under the door). More scenes and more animation.

```
TITLE: If Your ______ Ever ______… ______ (CAPS)

[0:00] If ______, ______. Don't ______. Don't ______. And whatever you do, don't ______.
[0:10] I'm not talking about ______. I'm not talking about ______. I'm talking about this.
[0:20] (Scene, present tense, 3 physical details) … (the viewer's test: "If ______, then ______.")
[0:45] (Threat line) … There are [5/7] rules. (Curiosity hook about rule 3 or the last rule.)
RULE 1  [easiest] Command / why / what it tries / if-then / sting
RULE 2  … + PLANT a question about the viewer's real life ("Keep that in mind.")
RULE 3  ["the one most people break"]
CASE STORY  (someone broke a rule; ends on one quiet detail)
RULE 4–5  escalation; it's testing you
RULE 6  the "it's over, isn't it?" fake-safe moment
RULE 7  the rule "nobody was supposed to need"
TWIST  pay off the plant; recontextualize; love or grief if possible
ENDING  "So if ______ … ______. But if ______, ______." + a 2–6 word final line
```
