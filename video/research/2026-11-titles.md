# Repetitive content risk and the next 60 titles (26 Nov 2026)

## 1. What YouTube actually says

Source: YouTube Help, "YouTube channel monetization policies" (support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392), read 26 Nov 2026.

- On 15 July 2025 YouTube renamed "repetitious content" to **"inauthentic content"**. It was a clarification, not a new ban. The rule decides whether a channel can **monetize**; it is not a strike or a removal policy.
- Reviewers judge the **whole channel**, looking at its main theme, most viewed videos, newest videos, biggest share of watch time, **metadata (titles, thumbnails, descriptions)** and About section. One bad pattern can cost monetization for the whole channel.
- "Generic or repetitive content" is content that "looks like it's made with a template, or that may feel repetitive to viewers after watching several videos in a row from the same channel" and "channels where content feels interchangeable from video to video".
- Not allowed (YouTube's own examples):
  - "Videos where characters are put in **the same situation over and over again with the same outcome** (i.e., using a highly similar storyline template across multiple videos)"
  - "Image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative, commentary, or educational value"
  - "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production"
  - "Content that repeatedly uses disturbing themes (such as violence or loss) without building a cohesive narrative", and "a series showing repetitive scenarios of animals in exaggerated distress or peril"
- Allowed: a series "in which each video has a distinct storyline, focus, or concept"; automated tools are fine if "the final product must still demonstrate your creative vision and provide educational or entertainment value".
- Separate **reused content** policy: using other people's material (clips, compilations, reading out others' text) without significant original commentary or changes. Our footage is licensed and short, inside our own narration, so this is low risk as long as we keep it that way.

**A title pattern on its own is not a violation.** Many large channels use one title formula. What gets channels demonetized is when the *videos themselves* feel interchangeable. The title is one signal a reviewer looks at, alongside the thumbnails and the content.

| Same music | Crocodile tracks reused on humpback and leopard | New music per video (owner supplies) |
| Danger and peril in every video | Attacks, "decides you're prey" | Mix in mystery, biology, history and record videos |
| Animal-in-peril scenes | Penguins and seals killed on screen | Keep it factual and part of the story, never the point of the video |

What protects us: original scripts with sources, our own animation, real-person inserts, a real story arc in each video, and checked facts. Keep those in every video.

- **M** myth or "we were wrong".
- **Q** mystery or question.
- **D** depth or journey.
- **B** biology or superpower.
- **V** versus, ranking or food chain.
- **P** prehistoric.
