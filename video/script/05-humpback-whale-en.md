# What Happens When a Humpback Whale Swallows You

English version of the approved Ukrainian video (`05-humpback-whale-uk.md`), written sentence for sentence so the same 497 shots line up with the narration. Facts as checked on 22 Nov 2026 (sources in the metadata). Visual notes: see the Ukrainian script; the visuals are the same shots.
Thumbnail text: YOU'RE IN ITS MOUTH..

---

## 1. Cold open

February 2025.

The Strait of Magellan, at the very bottom of South America.

A young man named Adrián is paddling a yellow kayak. His father is a few metres behind him, filming.

The water under Adrián turns dark.

And in one second, Adrián and his kayak are gone. Inside the mouth of a humpback whale.

So what really happens when a humpback whale swallows you? Can it actually swallow you? How long could you last in there? And why does this same whale risk its life to save other animals from killers?

## 2. Two seconds

For a moment there is nothing. Just a swirl on the water where a person used to be.

Adrián said later he thought he was already dead.

He felt something slimy close over his face, and everything went black.

Then, just as fast, the whale opened its mouth and let him go.

He popped up in the cold water, gasping. His father kept filming, and kept calling to him. Stay calm. Grab the boat.

Adrián reached his father's kayak. Both of them made it back to shore without a scratch.

The whole thing lasted a few seconds. But to understand what happened in those seconds, you need to know what grabbed him.

## 3. The animal

This is the humpback whale.

An adult is fourteen to seventeen metres long. Longer than a city bus.

It can weigh up to forty tonnes. That is more than six elephants.

Its flippers are the longest of any whale. Each one is up to five metres. A third of its body.

Those bumps on its head are not barnacles. Each one grows a single hair, like a whisker.

And it has no teeth at all. Not one.

So if it cannot bite, why does it need a mouth that big?

## 4. The mouth

Instead of teeth, it has baleen. Hundreds of plates hang from its upper jaw, like the teeth of a giant comb.

It hunts small things. Krill, tiny shrimp-like animals. And small fish.

To catch them, it charges at a swarm and opens its mouth wide.

Its throat is folded into long pleats, like an accordion.

When the water rushes in, the pleats stretch, and the throat balloons out like a bubble.

In a single gulp, it takes in tonnes of water.

Then it pushes the water out through the comb. And the food stays inside.

It does not look at each fish. It just swallows the whole patch. And sometimes, there is something else in that patch.

## 5. Lunge feeding

This is called lunge feeding.

The whale picks a spot, rises fast from the deep and opens its mouth right at the surface.

From above, you can see almost nothing. The water turns dark, and maybe starts to boil with fish.

Scientists who studied Adrián's video think that is exactly what happened.

The whale lunged at a school of fish. And Adrián was sitting right on top of it.

The whale never meant to grab him. It did not even know there was a person there until he was in its mouth.

And the moment it felt a hard plastic kayak, it spat everything back out.

But there is one man who was not so lucky. He did not get a few seconds. He got much longer.

## 6. Cape Cod, 2021

June 2021. The coast of Cape Cod, in Massachusetts.

Michael Packard is a lobster diver. He has done this for decades.

Right now he is about thirteen metres down, near the bottom.

Then something hits him. He said it felt like being hit by a truck.

Everything went black.

At first he thought it was a shark. Then he realised there were no teeth. And he felt the whale's muscles squeezing around him.

He was inside a humpback whale's mouth. With his breathing regulator still in.

He thought about his wife and his two sons. He was sure he was about to die.

## 7. Inside

So what was it like in there?

Dark. Tight. Wet. And everything moving. The whale was swimming and shaking its head.

He could still breathe from his tank. That was the only reason he had time to think.

Then the whale rose to the surface, opened its mouth and threw him into the air.

He fell into the water and stayed afloat. His crewmate pulled him into the boat.

He was in the whale's mouth for about thirty seconds. Maybe forty.

He went to hospital with bruises all over his body. Not a single broken bone. He went home the same day.

So he was in the mouth of a whale. But was he ever really in danger of being swallowed?

## 8. Could it swallow you?

Here is the answer to the first question.

A humpback's mouth is so big that a car would fit inside it. But its throat would not.

The opening to its stomach is about the size of your fist.

Even stretched to the limit, it only reaches thirty, at most thirty-eight centimetres. That is narrower than your shoulders.

It can take you in. But it cannot swallow you. Its body simply will not let you through.

Scientists have found seabirds in humpback whale droppings. Something small can slip down by accident. A person cannot.

So in the strict sense, no humpback whale has ever swallowed a person.

But there is one story that is more than a hundred years old. And it says a whale did.

## 9. The man in the whale

In 1891, newspapers told the story of a sailor named James Bartley.

His ship, the Star of the East, was hunting a whale near the Falkland Islands.

The whale smashed a boat, and Bartley disappeared.

The next day, the crew killed the whale and cut it open. And inside, they said, was Bartley. Alive. His skin bleached white by stomach acid.

The story went around the world. It is still told today.

But when a historian checked it a hundred years later, almost nothing fit.

The Star of the East was not even a whaling ship. And there is no James Bartley on the crew lists.

Even at the time, the captain's wife wrote that there was not one word of truth in the story.

And that whale was supposed to be a sperm whale. The one whale that really could swallow a person.

It is a myth. The real humpback is very different.

## 10. Bubble nets

Because humpbacks are not just big. They are clever.

In Alaska, they hunt in groups, and they have a special trick.

One whale dives under a school of fish and swims in a circle, blowing a stream of bubbles.

The bubbles rise like a curtain, and the fish are afraid to swim through it.

The circle tightens. The fish pack closer and closer.

Then the whales rise up together through the middle, mouths wide open.

This is a bubble net.

If you were in a boat above it, you would see a ring of bubbles appear on the calm water. A few seconds before the whales burst through it.

That ring is the warning. But very few people know how to read it.

## 11. The song

The males have one more skill. They sing.

They hang in the water, almost still, and make long, haunting sounds. Moans, groans and high squeals.

One song can last up to twenty minutes. Then they sing it again. For hours.

All the males in one area sing the same song. But the song keeps changing.

In the South Pacific, scientists watched new songs pass from whale to whale, from Australia east all the way to French Polynesia, in about two years.

No one knows for sure why they sing. Maybe to attract a female. Or to warn off other males.

Either way, for weeks the ocean is full of their music. And that makes what we did to them even harder to hear.

## 12. We nearly silenced them

For about a century, humpbacks were hunted almost everywhere they swam.

They are slow. They stay close to the coast. And they float when they die. That made them easy to take.

When commercial whaling finally stopped in 1985, most populations had fallen by more than ninety-five percent.

For every hundred whales, fewer than five were left.

Then people stopped. And something remarkable happened.

The humpbacks came back. In many oceans, there are tens of thousands again.

Which means more whales are feeding where people kayak, dive and swim.

That is why encounters like Adrián's are filmed more than ever.

## 13. The longest journey

And these whales travel further than almost any living thing.

Every year they swim from the cold feeding waters near the poles to the calm, warm waters near the tropics, where they give birth.

Some humpbacks swim more than eight thousand kilometres, one way. One of the longest migrations of any mammal on Earth.

In the warm water, there is almost nothing for them to eat. So for months, the mothers live off their own fat. And feed a growing calf.

Then they swim all the way back.

So the whale that grabbed Adrián was hungry. It was feeding after a journey like that.

But the strangest thing about humpbacks is not how they eat. It is who they protect.

## 14. The rescue

In 2009, in Antarctica, a scientist named Robert Pitman watched a pack of killer whales hunt a seal.

The seal was lying on a small piece of ice. The orcas swam at it together, making waves to wash it into the water.

Then two humpback whales arrived.

The seal slid into the water, right toward them.

And one whale rolled onto its back and lifted the seal onto its chest, out of the water, between its huge flippers.

When the seal started to slide off, the whale nudged it back with a flipper.

Then the seal swam to another piece of ice, and escaped.

Pitman could not believe it. So he started collecting other reports. And what he found was even stranger.

## 15. Why it fights killers

He found one hundred and fifteen cases of humpbacks meeting killer whales.

In most of them, the humpbacks charged in to break up the attack.

And most of the time, the victim was not a humpback. It was a seal. A sea lion. A grey whale calf. Even a fish.

Humpbacks are one of the few animals that fight back against orcas. Their flippers are covered in sharp barnacles, and one blow can wound.

So why save a seal?

Scientists think it may begin with protecting their own calves. They hear an orca attack and go, without checking who the victim is.

Whatever the reason, one of the largest animals in the sea spends its strength saving animals it does not need. It does not eat them. And it does not mate with them.

And you cannot help wondering what it felt when it found a person in its mouth.

## 16. If it happens to you

So how do you make sure this never happens to you?

Watch for the signs of feeding. Seabirds diving into one spot. Fish jumping out of the water. A ring of bubbles.

If you see them, paddle away from that spot. Do not move closer to get a better look.

Keep your distance. In many countries, the rule for boats is about a hundred metres from a whale.

If you dive where there are whales, take a surface buoy, so boats and whales can see where you are.

And if the worst happens and you end up in its mouth? Both survivors say the same thing: they had no time to do anything. The whale let them go on its own. The whale wants to be rid of you even more than you want to get out.

## 17. Close

So what happens when a humpback whale swallows you?

It won't. It can't.

For a few seconds, maybe half a minute, you will be in the mouth of one of the gentlest giants on Earth. In the dark. Squeezed. Sure that you are about to die.

And then it will do what it was always going to do.

It opens its mouth and lets you go.

The same animal that throws a person back into the sea will lift a seal out of it.

It was never hunting you. It just did not see you. This time it was a mistake. And it fixed it.
