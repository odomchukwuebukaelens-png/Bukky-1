## Description
It looks like it's smiling. It isn't.

In 2006, a photographer slipped under the Antarctic ice and a 3.5-metre female leopard seal swam straight at him, opened her jaws around his camera… and then spent four days trying to feed him penguins.

In this dive we look at the only seal that hunts other seals: the reptile-like head, the three-pointed teeth that work as both fangs and a krill sieve, why it seems to smile, the men of Shackleton's expedition who were chased across the ice in 1916, the scientist dragged under in 1985, the only known fatal attack in 2003, and the leopard seal that moved into a city marina in New Zealand. Plus: what to do if you ever meet one.
