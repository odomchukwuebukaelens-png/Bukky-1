## Description
In 1620, the Mayflower crossed the Atlantic. Somewhere beneath it, there may already have been a shark that is still swimming today.

The Greenland shark grows about a centimetre a year, swims slower than a stroll, and may live for around 400 years. In this video we follow the clues: how scientists found a clock hidden in its eye, how 1960s nuclear tests helped date it, the 392-year-old female, how the slowest fish ever measured still ends up with seals (and reindeer, and polar bears) in its stomach, the parasite hanging from its eye, the genome that keeps repairing itself, its poisonous meat, and the one found off Belize, thousands of kilometres from the Arctic.
